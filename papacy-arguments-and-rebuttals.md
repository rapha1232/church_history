## Part A: Todd Friel's "25 Reasons" and Trent Horn's rebuttals

The objections come from Todd Friel (*Wretched*), who counts down from 25 to 1. They are listed here from 1 to 25. Trent answers 14–16 together.

### 1. The papacy robs Jesus of his glory
- **Objection:** "It robs Jesus of his glory, which he will not give to another."
- **Rebuttal:** "God doesn't share his divine glory with anyone, but Catholics do not glorify the Pope or any other human person." Christ shares his *work* with his ministers.
- **References:** 1 Corinthians 3:9 ("For we are God's fellow workers"); Hebrews 13:17 ("Obey your leaders...").

### 2. The Church's bloody history
- **Objection:** "Jesus is not building his church on an institution that has a bloody and tawdry history."
- **Rebuttal:** "we should judge religion on its official teachings and its best examples, not caricatures or worst examples." Trent compares it with the "violence perpetrated by Protestants during Europe's wars of religion."

### 3. Corrupt doctrines (indulgences, Mary, saints, the Mass, the deuterocanonical books, works)
- **Objection:** "Why in the world would we believe that the office of the papacy was pure all the way back to Peter"
- **Rebuttal:** These are separate debates. Even if they were granted, "It says nothing about the legitimacy of the papacy."

### 4–5. "The Pope is the antichrist"
- Trent sums these up only as: "he just cites other Protestants who said that the Pope is the antichrist." He does not answer them one by one.

### 6. Jesus fought the Pharisees' legalism and would not install popes
- **Objection:** "Jesus did not fight to the death against one form of legalism, the Pharisees", only to put "another worse set of rulers, popes, in its place."
- **Rebuttal:** "Jesus's problem with the Pharisees wasn't their rules, it was their hypocrisy." Jesus even affirmed their seat of authority.
- **References:** Matthew 23:2–3 ("The scribes and the Pharisees sit on Moses's seat...").

### 7. Jesus and his teaching are the rock (Matthew 7)
- **Objection:** "Jesus considered himself the rock and his teachings the foundation of the church" ("Anyone who hears these words of mine...").
- **Rebuttal:** The rock metaphor means different things in different passages. In the parable "the rock is just a rock." In Matthew 16 it is tied to Peter.
- **References:** Matthew 7:24; Matthew 16; 1 Corinthians 11:26 (as given in the transcript).

### 8. Paul names only elders and deacons
- **Objection:** "Paul defines but two offices in the church: elders and deacon, not popes and cardinals."
- **Rebuttal:** "Paul doesn't have to outline the office of Pope because the Pope is an elder or a bishop." That is, the Pope is a bishop with a particular role.
- **References:** St. James as bishop of Jerusalem; the Council of Jerusalem (Acts 15).

### 9. Silence: the New Testament never calls Peter pope
- **Objection:** "silence testifies Peter wasn't a pope."
- **Rebuttal:** "Prior to his ascension into heaven, Jesus never tells anyone to write anything down." Not everything was written down, and the early Church shows apostolic succession.
- **References:** Clement of Rome, *1 Clement* 44: "Our apostles also knew through our Lord Jesus Christ..." (that there would be strife over the bishop's office).

### 10. The early Church honored the apostles' teaching, not the apostles
- **Objection:** "the early church honored the teaching of the apostles, but not the apostles themselves."
- **Rebuttal:** "this is clearly a case of honoring the apostles because of the authority Christ gave them."
- **References:** Acts 5:12–15 (Peter's shadow); Luke 10:16 ("whoever hears you hears me"); Ignatius of Antioch, c. AD 106: "You are subject to the bishop as to Jesus Christ" [*Letter to the Trallians* 2].

### 11. In the Old Testament the rock is God, not a man
- **Objection:** "the Old Testament, it identified the rock as divine, not human." Friel cites the Psalms and Isaiah 28:16 ("a tested stone").
- **Rebuttal:** "the Old Testament does not restrict the symbol of a rock to God alone." Abraham is called a rock. The keys echo the royal steward of the house of David.
- **References:** Isaiah 51:1–2 (Abraham as "the rock from which you were hewn"); Isaiah 22:22 (Eliakim, the key of the house of David); Matthew 16:19.

### 12. Paul scolded people for following Peter
- **Objection:** "Paul scolded ... the Corinthians, for giving devotion to a man, and that list included Peter himself."
- **Rebuttal:** The same list includes "I belong to Christ." So the fault is factionalism, not the authority of the person named: "Saying you belong to Peter is as bad as saying you belong to Christ."
- **References:** 1 Corinthians 1:10–12 ("Chloe's people").

### 13. Christ is the only foundation (1 Corinthians 3)
- **Objection:** "Paul made it clear again who the foundation is. I Corinthians 3."
- **Rebuttal:** "The fact that Christ is the church's ultimate foundation does not mean the church lacks a hierarchy built upon Christ."
- **References:** 1 Corinthians 3:1–11; 1 Corinthians 9:5; Galatians 2:9 (Peter, James and John as "pillars").

### 14–16. Christ is the cornerstone; Peter himself pointed to Christ
- **Objections:** (14) "Paul confirmed Ephesians chapter two, Jesus is the cornerstone, not Peter." (15) "even if Jesus were referring to Peter as the rock, what did Peter preach?" (16) "Peter understood who the rock is" (1 Peter 2: "a choice stone, a precious cornerstone").
- **Rebuttal:** Scripture uses building images in more than one way at once: "there is no contradiction in Peter being the rock upon which the church is built." The apostles are the foundation, Christ is the cornerstone, and the Church is the pillar.
- **References:** Ephesians 2:20; 1 Timothy 3:15; 1 Peter 2:4–8.

### 17. Peter refused glory (Acts 3)
- **Objection:** "Peter actually shunned the idea of receiving glory, Acts Chapter 3."
- **Rebuttal:** "Peter is demonstrating his holy humility by giving glory to God, which we'd expect the vicar of Christ to do."
- **References:** Acts 2–3; J.N.D. Kelly [*Oxford Dictionary of Popes*]: "Peter was the undisputed leader of the youthful church."

### 18. Peter called himself a "fellow elder," not pope
- **Objection:** "Peter didn't identify himself as the Pope when he called himself a bond servant and a fellow elder."
- **Rebuttal:** Humble self-description does not deny an office. Paul calls himself "the least of all the saints," and the absence of a title "does not disprove" a role.
- **References:** 1 Peter 5:1; 1 Corinthians 3:5; Ephesians 3:8; Matthew 10:2 (Peter "first," *protos*); John MacArthur (on *protos* meaning chief or leader).

### 19. Paul rebuked Peter (Galatians 2)
- **Objection:** "Paul actually rebuked Pope Peter in Galatians Chapter 2."
- **Rebuttal:** "Peter and Paul still agreed theologically. Paul rebukes Peter because the latter acted against his convictions." This was a failure of conduct, not of teaching.
- **References:** Galatians 2:11–14; Thomas Schreiner (Protestant commentator) [transcript: "Shriner"].

### 20. Peter denied Jesus three times
- **Objection:** "there was that whole denying the Lord three times business."
- **Rebuttal:** "Peter's denial of Jesus became an opportunity for Jesus to formally give ecclesial authority to St. Peter." The threefold "feed my sheep" mirrors the three denials.
- **References:** John 21:15–17; Bradford B. Blaine Jr. [*Peter in the Gospel of John*]; Herman Ridderbos [transcript: "Ritterbos"]; Joachim Jeremias.

### 21. The Pope's wardrobe is not humble
- **Objection:** "Have you seen the Pope's wardrobe? ... The greatest in the kingdom is the most humble, not the most pompous."
- **Rebuttal:** "You can be exalted and still be humble." Christ himself is exalted, and he promised the Twelve thrones.
- **References:** Matthew 18:4; Philippians 2:9–10; Matthew 19:28 / Luke 22:30 (twelve thrones); Jan Hus (quoted by Friel); Pope Francis.

### 22. The disciples asked who was greatest, so they didn't know Peter was
- **Objection:** "Why would they ask if they knew that Peter was the greatest in the kingdom?"
- **Rebuttal:** "the disciples didn't even have a full grasp of Jesus's Messianic mission." In Luke's parallel, Jesus answers with servant leadership, and that answer is reflected in the papal title *servus servorum Dei* ("servant of the servants of God"), used "since the 6th century."
- **References:** Matthew 18:1; Luke 22:24–32.

### 23. Jesus called Peter "Satan"
- **Objection:** "Shouldn't Jesus have known better than to call his vicar Satan?"
- **Rebuttal:** "Peter was assuming the traditional Jewish view that the Messiah would conquer rather than suffer." The rebuke corrects that misunderstanding and does not revoke the promise just made.
- **References:** Matthew 16:21–23; W.F. Albright and C.S. Mann [*Matthew*, Anchor Bible].

### 24. *Petros* and *petra* are a contrast, a play on words
- **Objection:** "Jesus' phrasing, it makes it really clear that he was making a contrast, a play on words."
- **Rebuttal:** Greek gender is grammatical: "the fact that a noun is masculine or feminine does not mean the thing in question is masculine or feminine." *Petra* had to become masculine *Petros* to serve as a man's name.
- **References:** Matthew 16:18; D.A. Carson [*Expositor's Bible Commentary*, Matthew].

### 25. Peter means "a little stone"
- **Objection:** "Jesus wasn't installing Peter as the first Papa. He was being clever." That is, *Petros* is a pebble and *petra* a boulder.
- **Rebuttal:** "modern Protestant Greek scholars admit there's no difference between the Greek words, Petros and Petra." Jesus also spoke Aramaic, where both words are *Kepha*.
- **References:** John 1:42 ("Cephas"); D.A. Carson; John Calvin; Oscar Cullmann (Lutheran) [transcript: "Oscar Coleman"; his work is *Peter: Disciple, Apostle, Martyr*]; William Cathcart, *The Papal System* (1872), the Protestant source Trent identifies behind the "pebble" argument; a 5th-century Syriac translation.

## Part B: Gavin Ortlund, "The Papacy Is Not From God," and Trent's response

> ⚠️ The published transcript stops partway through the section on Clement and Ignatius. Points 1–6 are verified from the verbatim text. Point 7 comes only from the page's summary and has not been checked against the audio.

### 1. Peter's commissions are only ordinary apostolic restoration
- **Ortlund:** "Peter is just being restored as an apostle to normal apostolic functions after his denials of Christ"
- **Trent:** "there's a wide number of views between Vatican one supremacy and normal apostolic functions." Even Protestant scholars grant Peter a unique primacy.
- **References:** Luke 22:31–32; John 21:15–17; Matthew 16:17–19; Isaiah 22:22; Matthew 10:2; Acts 10; Acts 15. D.A. Carson: "the argument for petrin primacy gains a certain plausibility". John MacArthur on *protos*: "It speaks of the chief, the leader of the group." J.N.D. Kelly: "Peter was the undisputed leader of the youthful church". Also Joachim Jeremias [transcript: "Achi Jeremiah"], David A. deSilva, and the *Dictionary of Jesus and the Gospels*. A scholar garbled as "Ernst tensions" [possibly Ernst Haenchen; unconfirmed] is quoted: "John 21 does not make Peter the bishop of Rome". Trent agrees and says the claim is about Peter's role, not the location.

### 2. Argument from silence: no enduring Petrine office in the New Testament
- **Ortlund:** If there were a Petrine office, "it'd probably come up somewhere"
- **Trent:** "we should be very careful about trying to divine particular ecclesial offices from these writings alone." The New Testament is not a church-order manual.
- **References:** Ephesians 4:11; Acts 1; Barnabas called an apostle; 2 Thessalonians 2:15 ("stand firm and hold to the traditions"); the Didache [transcript: "Diday show"]; Joe Heschmeyer, *Pope Peter*.

### 3. *Tu quoque:* the same silence argument hits Protestant church structures
- **Trent, citing Heschmeyer:** "scholars have found no trace whatsoever of any churches going" to a congregational or presbyterian structure. If silence defeats the papacy, it defeats those structures too.
- **References:** Leon Morris, *Evangelical Dictionary of Theology*; Acts 15.

### 4. Clement of Rome was just one elder among many
- **Ortlund (paraphrased by Trent):** *1 Clement* comes from a plurality of elders, not a single bishop with authority.
- **Trent:** "a chief church makes better sense of the data than no chief church". Rome intervened in Corinth, and that intervention was received. On Ortlund's source: "Duffy is a medieval historian, not an early church scholar".
- **References:** *1 Clement*; Philip Schaff (Protestant historian): Clement's letter is "the first example of the exercise of a sort of papal authority"; Eamon Duffy, *Saints and Sinners*; Edward Siecienski (Orthodox scholar) [transcript: "Zinsky"; his book is *The Papacy and the Orthodox*], in Trent's paraphrase: "Rome's preeminence is simply accepted by both Ignatius and presumably his readers without debate or explanation".

### 5. Ignatius proves "Antiochian supremacy" just as well
- **Ortlund:** "you could just as easily argue for Antiochian supremacy"
- **Trent:** "we can believe Ignatius had authority over the cluster of churches he wrote to because they were all nearby him". Only Rome is addressed as the church that "presides in love," and Ignatius gives Rome no orders.
- **References:** Ignatius, *To the Smyrnaeans* ("see that you all follow the bishop even as Jesus Christ does"); *To the Magnesians* ("your bishop presides in the place of God"); *To the Trallians* ("apart from these there is no church"); *To the Romans* (the church that "presides in love" [*prokathēmenē tēs agapēs*]; "As Peter and Paul issue commandments unto you"; Syria "which now has God for its shepherd").

### 6. Authority lies in the apostles' writings, not in successors
- **Trent:** "the apostolic writings tell us that the church's authority comes in the written word and the spoken word of the apostles." He also says "most scholars agree that the early church did not consider the New Testament to be inspired scripture until the time of [Irenaeus] at the end of the second century."
- **References:** 1 Timothy 3:15 ("the pillar and foundation of truth"); 1 Thessalonians 2:13; 2 Thessalonians 2:15; Acts 17:11 and 2 Timothy 3:16–17 (which Trent says refer to the Old Testament); Clement of Rome ("Scripture has nothing counterfeit in it"); Ignatius, who quotes apostolic writings but does not call them Scripture.

- **Fathers and popes:** Cyprian (*On the Unity of the Church*, its two editions; "every bishop shares Peter's chair"), Nicholas Afanasiev, Irenaeus (*Against Heresies* 3.3.2: every church must "agree with this church"), Pope Victor, Basil, Athanasius, Jerome and Pope Damasus, and Pope Agatho.

## Part C: Joe Heschmeyer's "most underrated argument" (Luke 22)

### The argument: Luke 22:24–32
- **Setup:** "the one that is worth mentioning the most is Luke 22, in which at the last supper, the disciples start debating which of them is the greatest." "Really I like to begin it with verse 24. The heart of it is verse 30 to 31."
- **Step 1, Jesus doesn't deny there is a greatest:** "he doesn't say no to their question. He doesn't say, 'Well, you're all equal. You're all equally great.'"
- **Step 2, greatness means service (vv. 25–27):** "to lead is to serve". "What makes the apostles distinct is they're called to be servant leaders over everyone."
- **Step 3, the Twelve receive thrones (vv. 28–30):** this "affirms that the apostles have a special authority, an eternal authority."
- **Step 4, from plural to singular (vv. 31–32):** "Satan has desired to sift *you*" is plural in Greek (Trent: "it's definitely plural"). Then the address turns singular to Peter: "But I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail and you have turned back, strengthen your brethren."
- **Conclusion:** "That's the heart of the papacy, the bishops and the successors through the apostles exist to serve the people of God, the church and the Pope exists to serve both the people of God, the church and even the other bishops." This is why the Pope is called "servant of the servants of God."

### Supporting points
1. **The classic texts:** Matthew 16:18–19 ("You are Peter... the keys of the kingdom") and John 21 ("Peter, do you love me? Feed my sheep").
2. **Why Peter doesn't advertise his role:** In 1 Peter he is "clothed with humility" (1 Peter 5:5). Mark, Peter's interpreter, "doesn't include all of Peter's good moments in his gospel". "What I mean to show by that is not therefore Peter bad, is like, therefore Peter's humble."
3. **No need to announce what was known:** "Because Peter's role was known and recognized in the early church, it was not necessary" to spell it out. None of the gospel writers announce themselves either. Joe cites Bart Ehrman on the anonymous gospels.
4. **Real papal authority is servant authority:** "frequently when the Papacy is at its strongest, the Pope isn't saying, 'Listen to me.'"
5. **Why it matters:** if the papacy is "of apostolic and divine origin, you should be Catholic."

### Objections answered
- **"If Peter were pope, why didn't Jesus say he's the greatest?"** Trent: "The amazing thing is if you actually read the full chapter, he almost does that."
- **The pope as a tyrant or king:** "They think the Pope is some king or a tyrant over the church." Joe: "that's just not the view." Protestant caricatures are "comically skewed."
- **Dispensationalism says the apostolic structure ended:** "Christ is very clear it doesn't." The Church is "an enduring institution." Darby, Scofield and Tim LaHaye's *Left Behind* are named.
- **Chapter divisions and section headers hide the link:** "The chapters and the verses and the headers and sub-headers are not found in the original text." Joe admits he "breezed right past some headers in the RSV." A similar example is the break between Revelation 11 and 12 (ark and woman).
- **"Why doesn't Peter just say, I'm the Pope, listen to me in his own letter?"** Humility (1 Peter 5). 1 Peter 5:13 also places Mark with Peter in "Babylon" (Rome).
