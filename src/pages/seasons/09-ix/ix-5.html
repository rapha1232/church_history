Toward reconciliation (1964–2026)

Starring

  • 5–6 January 1964. Pope Paul VI and Ecumenical Athenagoras I met in Jerusalem, the first meeting of a pope and an ecumenical patriarch since 1439.
  • 7 December 1965. A Joint Catholic–Orthodox Declaration was read simultaneously in Rome, at the last public session of Vatican II, and at the Phanar in Istanbul. Both sides regretted the offensive words and acts of 1054 and resolved to "remove both from memory and from the midst of the Church" the sentences of excommunication. The declaration explicitly stated that this act of justice and mutual pardon was not sufficient to end the differences between the Churches.1

Its first plenary was held on Patmos and Rhodes in 1980. It produced agreed statements on the Church and (Munich, 1982), faith and sacraments (Bari, 1987) and priesthood (Valamo, 1988).

In depth

The modern dialogue step by step

The first public sign of change was the meeting of Pope Paul VI and Ecumenical Patriarch Athenagoras I in Jerusalem in January 1964, the first meeting of a pope and an ecumenical patriarch since 1439. On 7 December 1965 they jointly declared that they 'regret' the offensive words and acts of 1054 and 'remove from memory' the excommunications (see the documents library).

A formal Joint International Commission for Theological Dialogue began in 1980. Its main documents are: Munich 1982 on Church, Eucharist and Trinity; Bari 1987 on faith and sacraments; Valamo 1988 on ordained ministry; Balamand 1993, which rejected 'uniatism' as a method of union for the future while recognising the Eastern Catholic Churches' right to exist; Ravenna 2007, which said that primacy and are interdependent at every level and that Rome held first place in the first millennium, while leaving its meaning open; and Chieti 2016 on synodality and primacy in the first millennium. Alexandria 2023 addressed synodality and primacy in the second millennium.

The Russian Orthodox Church withdrew from the Ravenna session over a dispute with Constantinople about Estonia, and broke communion with Constantinople in 2018 over Ukraine, which complicates any common Orthodox position.

Popes since John Paul II have described Catholics and Orthodox as 'sister Churches' and spoken of the Church breathing 'with both lungs'. Joint declarations have followed papal visits, including Benedict XVI in 2006, Francis in 2014, and the meeting of Leo XIV and Bartholomew at İznik (Nicaea) for the 1700th anniversary of the council in 2025.
