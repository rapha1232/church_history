How the families of Churches formed

Popular charts draw Christian history as a tree that splits cleanly at 431, 451 and 1054. The evidence supports a different picture. Each division has a zone of separation lasting decades or centuries, made up of contested councils, rival bishops, partial reconciliations and, eventually, distinct hierarchies. The diagram shows these zones as gradients rather than lines. Hover over or tap a band for details.

Original diagram for this edition. The gradients indicate the approximate period over which separate hierarchies and broken communion became established. They are interpretive, not exact.

  • Church of the East: separate jurisdiction from 410/424; formulation in 486; no of Ephesus.
  • Oriental Orthodox: rejection of Chalcedon from 451; separate hierarchy from c. 518–580.
  • Eastern Orthodox and Catholic: recurrent breaks (the Acacian schism 484–519, the Photian crisis 863–880); mutual excommunications in 1054 that targeted persons; a decisive popular estrangement after 1204; failed unions in 1274 and 1439; and a formal Orthodox rejection of Florence in 1484.
