Architecture and art in the West

Romanesque churches with thick walls and round arches (11th–12th c.) gave way to Gothic. Abbot Suger's rebuilt choir at Saint-Denis (dedicated 1144) is usually taken as the beginning. Rib vaults, pointed arches and flying buttresses allowed tall, light-filled cathedrals such as Chartres, Amiens and Cologne. Their stained glass and sculpture presented biblical history to their viewers. Giotto (d. 1337) and later Renaissance artists transformed religious painting. Church patronage was central to the Renaissance itself.

Byzantium, 1081–1453

Under the Komnenian emperors (1081–1185) Byzantium recovered, and the Church condemned the philosopher John Italos (1082) for an excessive use of Plato. After 1204 and the restoration of 1261, the empire shrank before the Ottomans. The Church nonetheless flourished in key respects :

  • Mount Athos (the Great Lavra was founded in 963) became the centre of Orthodox monasticism.1
  • The hesychast movement and Gregory Palamas (IX.4) shaped Orthodox spirituality, including the Jesus Prayer, later collected in the Philokalia (1782).2
  • Late Byzantine art, such as the mosaics and frescoes of the Chora monastery (c. 1315–21), shows the "Palaiologan renaissance".
  • Byzantine scholars, including Bessarion and Gemistos Plethon, carried Greek learning to Italy around the Council of Florence. Bessarion's library founded the Marciana in Venice.3

The fall of Constantinople (1453) placed the Ecumenical Patriarchate under Ottoman rule (XIII.3).

In depth

Byzantium's last centuries

The Komnenian emperors (1081–1185) restored Byzantine power for a century, but also brought Westerners into the empire as mercenaries and Venetian traders, whose privileges caused resentment. In 1182 Latins in Constantinople were massacred. The sack of 1204 and the Latin Empire (1204–1261) divided Byzantine lands; Greek successor states survived at Nicaea, Epirus and Trebizond, and Nicaea recovered Constantinople in 1261.

The restored Palaiologan state was small and poor, but its culture flourished. The mosaics and frescoes of the Chora monastery (Kariye) in Constantinople date from about 1315–1321. Mount Athos was the spiritual centre of Orthodoxy, linked with monasteries in Serbia, Bulgaria and Rus'.

The hesychast controversy (IX.4) was settled in favour of Gregory Palamas by councils of 1341–1351. Palamas distinguished between God's essence, which is unknowable, and his uncreated , in which humans can truly participate, so that monks who saw the 'light of Tabor' saw God himself. This distinction became characteristic of Orthodox theology and is still discussed in dialogue with the West.

Imperial efforts to win Western military aid through church union, at Lyon (1274) and Florence (1439), met strong resistance from clergy and people. Constantinople fell to Mehmed II on 29 May 1453. The last liturgy in Hagia Sophia was celebrated the night before; the church became a mosque.
