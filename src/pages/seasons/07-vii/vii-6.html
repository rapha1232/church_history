Two readings of the same evidence

Catholic reading

Christ gave Peter a unique role (Matt. 16:18–19; Luke 22:32; John 21:15–17) that continues in the bishops of Rome. The first millennium shows this in germ: Rome's interventions, appeals to it, the of its doctrinal letters, and Eastern appeals to it against heretical emperors. Development, as in the theology of Newman, explains how its later forms unfold what was implicit. Vatican I's definitions express what was always believed, even if not always exercised in the same way.1

Orthodox reading

Peter's confession (the faith) is the rock. Every bishop sits in the "chair of Peter" (Cyprian). Rome held the first place by the canons, because of its apostolic foundation and imperial dignity, and as a primacy within communion, not above it. The first millennium shows Rome's teaching accepted when orthodox and rejected when not, with ecumenical councils, not Rome alone, as the final authority. Later papal claims, from Gregory VII to Vatican I, are innovations.2

Both sides now accept that some forms of later doctrine cannot simply be read back into early texts. Both also accept that Rome held an acknowledged first place in the first millennium. The dispute concerns what that place entailed.3

The modern dialogue

  • 1995. John Paul II's encyclical Ut unum sint (§95) invited other Churches to help find a way of exercising the primacy that would be open to a new situation without renouncing what is essential to its mission.
  • 2007, Ravenna. The Joint International Commission for Theological Dialogue agreed that primacy and are interdependent at the local, regional and universal levels, and that Rome occupied the first place (taxis) among the patriarchates in the first millennium. It noted that the two Churches disagree on how to interpret the historical evidence. The Moscow Patriarchate was absent, having withdrawn over the presence of the Estonian Apostolic Orthodox Church.
  • 2016, Chieti. Synodality and Primacy during the First Millennium: a common historical reading of the first millennium, with Georgian reservations recorded.
  • 2023, Alexandria. Synodality and Primacy in the Second Millennium and Today.
  • 2025–2026. The Coordinating Committee worked on draft texts on the and on infallibility. In June 2026 at Pannonhalma it decided that the infallibility text required further revision before a plenary.4
