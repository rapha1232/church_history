Ephesus (431)

c. 4314 minPreviously: Constantinople I (381)

…the holy Synod decreed that it is unlawful for any man to bring forward, or to write, or to compose a different ( ἑτέραν ) Faith as a rival to that established by the holy Fathers assembled with the Holy Ghost in Nicæa.

Starring

A. Historical context
Nestorius' objections to the title Theotokos (428); Cyril's campaign; Celestine's Roman synod (430). Narrative in Vol. IV ch. 11.
B. Participants
About 150–200 bishops in Cyril's assembly, with Memnon of Ephesus and the Asian bishops. About 43 in John of Antioch's counter-council. The Roman legates were the bishops Arcadius and Projectus and the presbyter Philip. Nestorius was in the city but refused to attend.1
C. Political context
Theodosius II summoned the council. His commissioner Candidian opposed the opening of 22 June. Court factions, the empress Pulcheria (sympathetic to Cyril), and gifts to officials shaped the outcome.
D. Theological issues
Whether the eternal Word is the single subject of the incarnate life, so that Mary is truly Theotokos; how to speak of the union of divinity and humanity.
E. Competing arguments
Cyril: the Word himself was born, suffered "in the flesh" and died; to deny this divides Christ and empties salvation. Nestorius: the divine nature cannot be born or suffer; Scripture attributes these things to the man; Christ is one prosopon of union of two complete natures; Theotokos risks pagan "mother goddess" language and Apollinarianism.
F. Decisions
Deposition of Nestorius. Approval of Cyril's Second Letter to Nestorius as consonant with Nicaea; the status of the Third Letter and Twelve Anathemas is debated. Canon 7 against "a different faith". Condemnation of the Messalians and of Pelagian supporters.
G. Reception
Recognised as ecumenical by Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox Churches. Rejected by the Church of the East. Its reception depended on the reunion of 433, and Chalcedon later read Ephesus as including the Formula of Reunion.2
H. Consequences
Precedent for the term Theotokos and for single-subject Christology. Grievance in the Antiochene and Persian Churches. The question of "two natures" was left open and re-erupted in 448.
I. Primary sources
Q14 (Cyril's first anathema); Q15 (sentence against Nestorius); canon 7:

…the holy decreed that it is unlawful for any man to bring forward, or to write, or to compose a different (ἑτέραν) Faith as a rival to that established by the holy Fathers assembled with the Holy Ghost in Nicæa.

Ephesus, canon 7. Trans. Percival, NPNF II/14 (1900). The opening was checked; the final clause follows the same translation. Q16 · partly checked
J. Historical controversies
Whether Cyril's assembly was a valid council or a partisan synod; whether Nestorius actually taught "two sons". His later Book of Heracleides, rediscovered in Syriac in 1889, shows him accepting much of Chalcedon's language.3 Whether the Twelve Anathemas were formally adopted.
K. Legacy
Theotokos became the defining Marian title of the Chalcedonian and Oriental Orthodox traditions (Key terms). The council remains the main point of separation from the Church of the East, a separation softened by the 1994 declaration.

In depth

The Council of Ephesus (431): a council in two halves

Nestorius, a monk of Antioch, became bishop of Constantinople in 428. He objected to calling Mary ('God-bearer'), preferring Christotokos ('Christ-bearer'), because he wanted to avoid saying that God was born or suffered. Cyril of Alexandria answered with letters and twelve anathemas insisting that the Word himself was born of Mary according to the flesh.

Pope Celestine condemned Nestorius at a Roman synod in 430. Emperor Theodosius II then summoned a council to Ephesus for Pentecost 431. On 22 June, Cyril opened it before John of Antioch and the Syrian bishops had arrived, and some 150 bishops deposed Nestorius the same day. Crowds in the city celebrated with torches.

When John arrived a few days later, his own assembly deposed Cyril and Memnon of Ephesus. The papal legates joined Cyril's side. The emperor at first upheld both depositions and held the leaders in custody, before letting Cyril return to Alexandria. Nestorius retired to a monastery and was later exiled to Egypt, where he wrote a defence, the Bazaar of Heracleides, discovered in a Syriac version in 1895.

Peace came in 433 with the Formula of Reunion, a statement drafted by the Antiochenes and accepted by Cyril, which confessed Mary as Theotokos and spoke of 'a union of two natures'. The churches of Persia, which later formed the Church of the East, never accepted Ephesus.
