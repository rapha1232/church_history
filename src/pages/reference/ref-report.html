Coverage

  • Chronological: from the emergence of Israel (c. 1200 BC) to 2026, in fifteen volumes. The densest coverage is c. 30–1500; Volumes X–XV are survey-level treatments of very large periods.
  • Geographical: the Roman/Byzantine Mediterranean, the Latin West, Persia/Mesopotamia, Armenia, Georgia, Egypt, Ethiopia, Central Asia, China and India. Europe, the Americas, Africa and Asia in Volumes X–XV. Coverage of Nubia, Arabia and the Pacific is thin.
  • Scope now complete in outline: all fifteen volumes, Part III (profiles and a 24-topic matrix for 12 traditions) and Part IV (studies A–L). Thinner areas: Latin American, Asian and African church history after 1900 at national level; Oriental Orthodox and Eastern Catholic history after 1500 in detail; the history of women beyond named figures; Orthodox and Protestant canon law; the full text of the Maronite liturgy and synods.

Important limitation. The secondary scholarship in the bibliography was not opened during preparation. Statements attributed to modern scholars (Barnes, Hanson, Williams, Ayres, Brock, Dvornik, Runciman, Brubaker and Haldon, and others) and the status labels "consensus" and "contested" reflect the compiler's knowledge of that literature. They should be checked against the works themselves before being cited. For the same reason, secondary works are cited by chapter or in general, never by invented page numbers.

Edition 4: fact-check, footnotes and source standards

The whole edition was reviewed chapter by chapter. Chapters with few notes were given footnotes to primary texts (Scripture, ancient authors by book and chapter, council canons, papal documents by Denzinger–Hünermann number, confessions of faith) and to peer-reviewed or university-press scholarship. Every chapter now ends with a Sources & quick links box listing the online texts, the works cited and the register entries for that chapter. Every bibliography entry now carries a label for its kind of source; the policy is set out under Method and sources.

Online links were included only after the page was opened and found to contain the named text. Bibliographic details were checked online for the Stanford Encyclopedia entries and the official documents. The other newly added works are standard titles whose details are given from the compiler’s knowledge and should be confirmed before citation. The modern secondary works themselves were not re-read, so they are cited as whole works or by volume, without chapter or page numbers.

Corrections made during review

  • Edition 4: the Formula of Hormisdas was not signed by “some 250 Eastern bishops”; Patriarch John II signed on 28 March 519, followed by most Eastern bishops.
  • Edition 4: Trent closed with 255 signatories, about 200 of them bishops (previously “about 200 signed”).
  • Edition 4: Thirty Years War mortality restated as perhaps a fifth of the population of the Empire, following Wilson (2009).
  • Edition 4: Lateran IV, canon 1, uses the participle transsubstantiatis, not the noun; the wording was corrected.
  • Edition 4: leaders of the Assyrian and Ancient Churches of the East added; the Catholic membership figure updated to the Annuarium Statisticum Ecclesiae 2023 (1.406 billion).
  • Edition 4: register entry Q39 now cites the official text rather than news reports.
  • Cyril's first anathema and the Ephesus sentence on Nestorius were re-quoted from the edition text after a first draft misattributed the subject of "decrees".
  • The attribution of Constantinople I canon 3 was verified through the Chalcedon acts and Socrates, because the online canons page could not be read in full.
  • Irenaeus 3.3.2: the paraphrase of the "very tedious" passage was corrected to the exact wording, and the Latin variant potiorem was noted.
  • The page reference for Humbert's bull in Will (1861) was removed because it could not be checked.
  • Claims about Peter Olivi and papal infallibility, the Trullan canons' reception in Rome, and the 2018 Moscow–Constantinople break were qualified or reworded.
  • Edition 3: a scholarly attribution in the Maronite chapter was removed because it could not be confirmed; an unconfirmed statement about the leadership of the Ancient Church of the East was withdrawn; the theses of Luther are quoted in Latin (public domain) with a translation by this edition instead of a copyrighted English version; the regional chart in XV.8 shows only the two percentages that were verified, plus the remainder.

Disputed questions flagged in the text

These include: the nature of Constantine's conversion; the date of Arius' condemnation; the origin of the 381 creed; "in" or "from" two natures at Chalcedon; the authenticity of the Origenist canons of 553; the Honorius question; the origins of iconoclasm; the Filioque at Toledo 589; the readings of Irenaeus 3.3.2 and of Ignatius' "presiding in love"; the meaning of the Pentarchy; and the date of the schism. Each is marked where it occurs.

Methodology and review criteria

Each chapter was written against the requirements of the project brief: historical accuracy; separating history, tradition and doctrine; verified quotations; fairness to each tradition; and avoiding anachronism. The edition was then reviewed for chronology (dates cross-checked between chapters, the timeline and the register), terminology (glossary consistency) and denominational fairness. For each disputed point, the test applied was whether a scholar of the relevant tradition would recognise the description.
