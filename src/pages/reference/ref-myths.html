Common misconceptions

Each claim below is widely repeated. Each verdict is based on the evidence discussed in this edition.

“Constantine made Christianity the official religion of the Roman Empire.”

Incorrect

Constantine granted toleration and lavish patronage. Nicene Christianity became the official religion by law under Theodosius I (Cunctos populos, 380), and pagan sacrifice was banned in 391–392. See IV.2 and IV.9.

“The Edict of Milan made Christianity the state religion.”

Incorrect

The text of 313 granted freedom of worship to Christians "and all others" and restored church property. See IV.3.

“The Council of Nicaea decided which books belong in the Bible.”

No evidence

No ancient source connects Nicaea with the biblical canon. The claim appears to originate in a much later story, the Synodicon Vetus legend of books placed on an altar, and in early modern polemic. Canon lists were discussed at regional synods (e.g. Laodicea, c. 363, whose list in canon 60 may be a later addition; Hippo 393; Carthage 397) and in individual writers such as Athanasius' Festal Letter 39 (367). See IV.6.

“The Trinity was invented in 325.”

Misleading

Christians baptised and prayed in the threefold name from the first century (Matt. 28:19; the Didache). The technical vocabulary (homoousios, one ousia in three hypostases) was developed between 325 and 381, and the doctrine of the Spirit was formalised in 381. Nicaea defined the Son's full divinity against Arius; it did not create belief in Father, Son and Spirit. See IV.8.

“Jesus' divinity was decided by a close vote at Nicaea.”

Incorrect

The Son's divinity was not in question between the parties. Arius also called him God, in a derived sense. The issue was whether he was eternal and of the same being as the Father. Only two bishops refused to sign. See IV.6.

“St Nicholas slapped Arius at Nicaea.”

Later legend

Nicholas of Myra is not on the earliest subscription lists of Nicaea. The story appears only in medieval and later hagiography.1

“The Church split into Catholic and Orthodox in 1054.”

Oversimplified

1054 saw personal excommunications that were not intended to break communion between Churches. Separation was a process from the 9th to the 15th century, with 1204 as a turning point in popular feeling and 1484 as a formal Orthodox rejection of union. See IX.2 and IX.7.

“The 1965 declaration ended the schism.”

Incorrect

It removed the excommunications of 1054 from memory and explicitly said this was not sufficient to restore communion. See IX.5.

“The Church of the East is the Church founded by Nestorius.”

Incorrect

The Persian Church organised itself in 410 and declared jurisdictional independence in 424, before Ephesus. Its theology derives mainly from Theodore of Mopsuestia. "Nestorian" is a polemical label. See V.1.

“The Oriental Orthodox are Monophysites who deny Christ's humanity.”

Incorrect

They anathematise Eutyches and confess Christ's humanity as complete and consubstantial with ours. Their formula is Cyril's "one incarnate nature". See V.3.

“All Eastern Churches are Eastern Orthodox.”

Incorrect

Eastern Christianity includes the Eastern Orthodox, the Oriental Orthodox, the Assyrian and Ancient Churches of the East, and over twenty Eastern Catholic Churches in communion with Rome. See V.2.

“The early Church was governed by five patriarchs from the beginning.”

Anachronistic

The Pentarchy took shape between 381 and the sixth century. Jerusalem became a patriarchate only in 451. See VII.5.

“The papacy as defined in 1870 existed unchanged from Peter” / “Rome had no special role before the Middle Ages.”

Both oversimplified

Rome held an acknowledged first place and exercised influence from the late first century. The form and extent of its authority developed and were contested. See VII.1 and VII.6.
