How the canon formed

No council in the first centuries handed down a list from nowhere: the churches read the books they had received, argued over a few, and slowly converged. These are the lists that mark the way, from the second century to the Reformation and beyond.

  1. c. 150

    Melito of Sardis Greek East

    The earliest Christian list of the Old Testament, gathered in Palestine: the Hebrew books except Esther (Wisdom of Solomon may be named).

  2. c. 180

    Muratorian Fragment Latin West

    A Latin list probably from Rome (some date it to the 4th century). Includes Jude, two letters of John, Revelation and the Wisdom of Solomon; Hermas may be read but not in church; omits Hebrews, James and the letters of Peter.

    2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Shepherd of Hermas Wisdom

  3. c. 240

    Origen Greek East

    Reported by Eusebius: accepts the four Gospels and most letters, notes doubts over 2 Peter, 2–3 John, and the authorship of Hebrews; uses Hermas and Barnabas highly.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Shepherd of Hermas Epistle of Barnabas

  4. c. 325

    Eusebius, Church History 3.25 Greek East

    Divides books into acknowledged, disputed (James, Jude, 2 Peter, 2–3 John) and spurious (Hermas, Barnabas, Didache). Revelation appears in both the acknowledged and the spurious lists.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Shepherd of Hermas Epistle of Barnabas Didache

  5. c. 350

    Cyril of Jerusalem Greek East

    Catechetical Lecture 4: 22 Old Testament books including Baruch and the Letter of Jeremiah; 26 New Testament books, without Revelation.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Baruch

  6. c. 363

    Council of Laodicea (canon 60) Greek East

    A list attributed to the council (its authenticity is debated): no Revelation; Baruch and the Letter included with Jeremiah.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Baruch

  7. c. 367

    Athanasius, 39th Festal Letter Greek East

    The first list of exactly the 27 New Testament books. Old Testament: the Hebrew books with Baruch and the Letter, without Esther. Wisdom, Sirach, Esther, Judith, Tobit, the Didache and Hermas are to be read by catechumens but are not canonical.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Shepherd of Hermas Didache Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch

  8. c. 382

    Council of Rome (Damasus) Latin West

    A list in the later Decretum Gelasianum, attributed to Damasus’ synod (attribution debated): the full 73-book list as later defined at Trent.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Maccabees

  9. c. 391

    Jerome, Prologue to Kings Latin West

    Prefers the Hebrew canon; Wisdom, Sirach, Judith, Tobit and Maccabees are “apocrypha” (outside the canon) but may be read for edification. He nonetheless translated several of them.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Maccabees

  10. c. 397

    Councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397, 419) Latin West

    African councils, with Augustine present: the full list, including Tobit, Judith, Wisdom, Sirach and 1–2 Maccabees, and the 27 New Testament books (Hebrews counted with Paul’s letters by 419).

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  11. 405

    Innocent I to Exsuperius Latin West

    A papal letter giving the same list as Carthage.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Maccabees

  12. 692

    Council in Trullo (Quinisext) Greek East

    Endorses several earlier lists at once (Apostolic Canons, Laodicea, Carthage, Athanasius), leaving the Eastern boundary somewhat open.

  13. 730

    John of Damascus Greek East

    Exposition of the Orthodox Faith 4.17: 22 Old Testament books; Wisdom and Sirach are “admirable and good” but not counted; 27 New Testament books, with the Apostolic Canons.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Wisdom Sirach

  14. 1442

    Council of Florence Latin West

    The Decree for the Copts lists the full Catholic canon.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  15. 1534

    Luther’s Bible Protestant

    The Apocrypha printed between the Testaments, “not held equal to Holy Scripture, but useful and good to read”. Hebrews, James, Jude and Revelation placed at the end of the New Testament.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  16. 1546

    Council of Trent Catholic

    Defines the canon dogmatically “with all their parts” as in the Vulgate, anathematising those who reject it. 1–2 Esdras and the Prayer of Manasseh are placed in an appendix.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  17. 1571

    Thirty-Nine Articles (Art. VI) Protestant

    Anglicans read the Apocrypha “for example of life and instruction of manners” but not “to establish any doctrine”, citing Jerome.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  18. 1646

    Westminster Confession Protestant

    Lists the 66 books; the Apocrypha “are of no authority in the Church of God” and no more than other human writings.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  19. 1672

    Synod of Jerusalem Orthodox

    Against Calvinist influence (Cyril Lucaris), affirms Wisdom, Judith, Tobit, the history of the Dragon, Susanna, Maccabees and Sirach as “genuine parts of Scripture”.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  20. 1826

    British and Foreign Bible Society Protestant

    Decides to stop funding Bibles that include the Apocrypha; most English Protestant Bibles omit them from then on.

    Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees

  21. 1900

    Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church Oriental Orthodox

    The broadest canon, often counted as 81 books, including 1 Enoch and Jubilees.

    Hebrews James 2 Peter 2 John 3 John Jude Revelation Tobit Judith Wisdom Sirach Baruch Maccabees
