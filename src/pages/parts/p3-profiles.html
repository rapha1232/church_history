Profiles of the traditions

Starring

Roman Catholic Church

Origins: claims continuity with the apostolic Church through the see of Rome; history in Vols II–XV. Leadership: the pope with the college of bishops; Code of Canon Law (1983). Worship: Roman Rite Mass (and other Latin rites); seven sacraments. Doctrine: 21 ecumenical councils; Catechism of the Catholic Church (1992). Scripture: 73 books. Distribution: 1.406 billion baptised members at the end of 2023 (Annuarium Statisticum 2023); largest communities in Latin America, Europe, Africa and the Philippines.

Eastern Catholic Churches

23 Churches sui iuris in full communion with Rome, following Byzantine, Alexandrian, Antiochene, Armenian and East Syriac traditions: for example the Ukrainian Greek Catholic, Syro-Malabar, , and Chaldean Churches. Law: Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches (1990). See XIII.6. Together perhaps 18–20 million members.

Eastern Orthodox Church

Origins: the Byzantine imperial Church of the first millennium; separated from Rome over centuries (Vol. IX). Leadership: autocephalous Churches (14–17 depending on recognition), each with its ; the Ecumenical is "first among equals". Worship: the Byzantine Divine Liturgy; seven or more "mysteries" (the number is not dogmatically fixed). Doctrine: seven ecumenical councils; Holy Tradition. Distribution: commonly estimated at about 220–260 million; Russia, Ukraine, Romania, Greece, Serbia, Bulgaria, Georgia, the Middle East and the .1

Oriental Orthodox Churches

Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian, Eritrean and Malankara Orthodox Churches. They accept three councils and confess Christology (V.2). In communion with one another, each autocephalous. Ancient liturgies in Coptic, Syriac, Armenian and Ge'ez. Commonly estimated at about 60–80 million, of whom the Ethiopian Church forms the largest part.

Assyrian Church of the East

The historic Church of the East (V.1). -Patriarch (Mar Awa III since 2021), seat in Erbil since 2015. East Syriac liturgy (Addai and Mari). Accepts the councils of 325 and 381. Members in Iraq, Iran, Syria, India and a large diaspora; total commonly given as several hundred thousand.

Ancient Church of the East

Separated from the Assyrian Church in 1968, over the calendar and hereditary succession. Seat in Baghdad. Reunion talks with the Assyrian Church have taken place at intervals; the most recent failed in 2022. Catholicos-Patriarch Gewargis III Younan was elected in November 2022 and consecrated in Baghdad on 9 June 2023.

Lutheran Churches

Origins: Wittenberg Reformation (XI.2). Confessions: Book of Concord (1580). Polity: varied; episcopal in Scandinavia and the Baltic (some in historic succession), synodal elsewhere. Worship: liturgical Divine Service; two sacraments, with confession valued. World bodies: Lutheran World Federation; the more conservative International Lutheran Council (incl. the Missouri Synod). Largest growth in Ethiopia (Mekane Yesus) and Tanzania.2

Anglican Communion

Origins: the English Reformation (XI.5). Instruments of unity: the Archbishop of Canterbury, the Lambeth Conference, the Anglican Consultative Council and the Primates' Meeting. Provinces are autonomous. Formularies: Thirty-Nine Articles, Book of Common Prayer and Ordinal; the Chicago–Lambeth Quadrilateral (1886/88). Distribution: the largest provinces are in Africa (Nigeria, Uganda). Divided since 2003–08 over sexuality (XV.8).3

Reformed and Presbyterian Churches

Origins: Zurich and Geneva (XI.3). Confessions: Heidelberg, Belgic, Dort, Westminster and others. Polity: presbyterian, with ministers and elders in sessions, presbyteries and synods; Congregationalists are a related tradition. Worship: centred on preaching; two sacraments. World bodies: World Communion of Reformed Churches; also the more conservative World Reformed Fellowship. Large communities in Korea, Indonesia, southern Africa, Brazil and Hungary.

Methodist / Wesleyan Churches

Origins: the Wesleys' revival (XIV.3). Polity: connexional, governed by conferences; bishops in the US tradition. Doctrine: Arminian; assurance; sanctification; Wesley's Sermons and Notes; Articles of Religion (1784). Includes the Holiness churches, such as the Nazarenes. World body: World Methodist Council.4

Baptist Churches

Origins: English Separatists (1609–12) (XI.5). Distinctives: believers' baptism by immersion; congregational autonomy; religious liberty; "soul competency". There are General (Arminian) and Particular (Calvinist) streams. World body: Baptist World Alliance. The Southern Baptist Convention, the largest US Protestant denomination, left the BWA in 2004.

Anabaptist Churches

Mennonites, Amish, Hutterites and Brethren (XI.4). Believers' baptism, discipleship, nonviolence and the community of faith. World body: Mennonite World Conference; the largest national churches are now in Africa (DR Congo, Ethiopia) and India. Total about 2 million.

Pentecostal Churches

Origins: Topeka and Azusa Street (XV.3). Distinctives: baptism in the Holy Spirit, spiritual gifts, divine healing, expectation of Christ's return. Classical denominations (Assemblies of God, Church of God in Christ, Foursquare) plus many independent churches. Oneness Pentecostals are non-Trinitarian. Largest presence in Latin America, Africa and Asia.

Seventh-day Adventist Church

Origins: Millerite movement; organised 1863 (XIV.5). Distinctives: Saturday Sabbath; Christ's imminent return; the heavenly sanctuary and investigative judgement; conditional immortality (no conscious existence after death until the resurrection); health message; the prophetic ministry of Ellen G. White. Polity: General Conference. About 22–23 million baptised members (church statistics).5

Independent and other movements

African Initiated Churches (Kimbanguist, Aladura, Zion Christian Church); Chinese house churches; non-denominational and "network" churches; Quakers (Religious Society of Friends); Churches of Christ; Old Catholics; and others. Movements that reject Nicene Trinitarian doctrine, such as the Latter-day Saints and Jehovah's Witnesses, are noted in XIV.5.
